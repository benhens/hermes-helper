# Helper

A Hermes Desktop plugin that helps you understand Hermes and set it up the way you want, one step at a time. It explains and checks; it never installs, builds or changes settings for you.

Status: early build (Setup steps 1-4, Helper's conversation window, Helper's page in Settings, and the "Your setup" checks). Helper's conversation answers many messages from pre-written lines chosen by fixed rules on your computer. When those rules are not sure, the AI your Hermes uses writes the reply from Helper's checked lines, and fixed rules check it before it is shown (point 11).

## What it adds

- One row in Hermes's left sidebar: **Helper**.
- One page with two tabs: **Setup** (steps, one at a time) and **Your setup** (checks of your own Hermes).
- **Your setup** shows seven cards, each with one status (Works, Needs a fix, Not set up yet, Not checked yet, Can't check right now), the fixed rule it passes by, and what to do next: Hermes is connected, a model is connected, the model answers, the personality file (SOUL.md), skills, the memory switch, and a memory test you run in your own chat. With more than one bot (profile), a picker chooses which bot the cards show. Fixed rules in code decide every status; no AI judges anything.
- **Setup** has four steps: what you want Hermes to do, the brain (models), where Hermes runs, and connecting things (tools, skills, plugins, MCP). Each step explains in plain words, shows what the official Hermes docs say with a link to each page, and asks one question with answer cards. "I understand, next step" moves on; your answers are kept. Steps 5-7 are listed as coming later.
- Helper's conversation window on the right. Its character sits on the top edge of the message box: two eyes and a mouth in the theme's accent colour, holding on with its fingers. It comes up from behind the box when the page opens, its eyes follow your cursor while it is over this page (or the field the keyboard is on), and it shows when it is busy, asking or talking. Helper's newest sentence is in a speech bubble above the character and appears word by word; press a key or the mouse to see the rest at once. When Helper asks your AI, three dots show in the speech bubble while it waits, for at most 12 seconds. If your AI is slow, not set up, or its reply fails Helper's checks, Helper shows its own pre-written line instead. If your AI is too slow, Helper stops asking it for the rest of the visit. When that happens with the first message Helper sends it in a visit, Helper says so once, with a text you can paste into your own Hermes to make it quicker. Otherwise it says nothing about it. When Helper asks your AI, three dots show in the speech bubble while it waits, for at most 12 seconds. If your AI is slow, not set up, or its reply fails Helper's checks, Helper shows its own pre-written line instead, and says nothing about it. Older lines move up as small bubbles, your own messages are on the right, and the conversation scrolls when it is full. Only some questions come with answers to pick (which AI, which chat app, which service, "which one do you mean?", and Yes / No before deleting): they appear inside the message box, above the line where you type, once Helper's line is said, and go away when you pick one or send a message; you can always type instead. Ask "what can I ask?" to see the four topics. Some lines hold a button: one opens a text to paste into a chat with your own Hermes, one opens Hermes's own Messaging, Model or Capabilities screen. If you ask Helper to do something for you (install, connect, set up), it gives you a text to paste into a new chat with your own Hermes instead, because Helper never changes anything itself. With your system's "reduce motion" setting on, the character stays still, each line appears whole and the answers appear at once.
- What Helper keeps about you (your name, three kinds of answers, up to three things you ask it to remember): each time it keeps something, a small line says so ("Kept: ...") with a "See what I keep" link; your name is kept as soon as you give it. Helper asks your name only on your very first visit: after that, also when you pressed Skip or used "Forget all of this", it opens with "Hi again" (by name when one is kept) and asks nothing; type "my name is ..." to change the name. Ordinary talk gets ordinary lines: "hey", "thanks", "ok", or a message that looks cut off ("he") never gets the "I don't know" line, and Helper never says the same line twice in a row. Say "my chat app is WhatsApp" (or the AI or service you use) and the newest replaces the one kept. Type "what do you know about me" to see the list, or "forget my name" (it asks Yes or No first). Once something is forgotten or replaced, its older "Kept: ..." line is struck through.
- Using fewer tokens: ask in your own words ("how can I use less usage?") and Helper says long chats use the most and where "New session" is; its button "See what uses them" says to type /usage in a Hermes chat.
- Helper's own page in Hermes's Settings (Settings, then Plugins, then Helper): what Helper keeps, as a short text you can edit, one thing per line. Save reads it back by fixed rules and asks before it forgets anything; "Forget all of this" deletes it all.
- What Hermes can do, checked against its docs: "What Hermes can do" shows four picks; "Web, files, pictures and voice" asks what you want Hermes to do, with four groups (Search and use the web, Files and my computer, Pictures and video, Voice and sound), and a group shows what Helper has checked for it: 26 things in all, three at a time with "More" when there are more than four. Each says what Hermes has for it (a built-in tool, a skill that comes with Hermes, or an optional skill you add first) and gives a text to ask your own Hermes, or, for an optional skill, the clicks in the Hermes app to add it. Typed questions reach them too ("can it read PDFs"). Ask about a skill by its name next to the word "skill" ("is there a github skill", "what does the dspy skill do") and Helper says whether Hermes comes with it or you add it first and, in plain words, what it teaches Hermes (174 of the 186 skills; the other 12 keep a fixed sentence), with the official docs' own description in a box headed "From the Hermes docs". A skill name that is also an ordinary word (shop, maps, sketch, 19 in all) counts after "skill for" only as the last word ("is there a skill for shop", not "skills for shop owners"). Asked "is there a skill for knitting", Helper says it does not know rather than explaining what a skill is. Where nothing is checked but the question is close to a group, Helper says it does not know and offers that group's nearest things. Gmail gets the way to the app's Connectors page, and Google Calendar and Google Drive how to look for themselves there; GitHub gets the github skill. Asked to build something ("make me a website"), or whether Hermes can ("can it build a website?"), Helper gives the text to paste into your own Hermes. Two-word names win over their single words ("google drive", "google mail", "one drive" is OneDrive), also when typed as the answer to "Which service?". Every docs line Helper's checked answers quote is checked against the Hermes v0.23.0 docs when the plugin is built.
- Drag the window's left edge to make it between 360 and 600 px wide (or use the arrow keys on it); the width is remembered.
- Narrow windows: first the step list shows only its numbers, then Helper's window narrows to 360 px. When the page is too narrow for both sides, Helper lies over the page with a thin strip at the page's right edge; press the strip (or Escape) to fold Helper away, and the strip again to bring it back. Side-by-side text and the "Your setup" cards go to one column.

## What it reads, sends and changes

- Reads your Hermes setup only through Hermes's own read-only calls: whether a model is ready and its name (`setup.runtime_check`, `setup.status`, `free_tier.status`), your SOUL.md text (`profiles.describe`), which skills and toolsets are on, and your bot names. It never reads `.env`, `auth.json` or your config file.
- The "The model answers" card makes one call to your own model through Hermes, and only when you press Check. It asks the model to reply "OK", which spends a little of your model credit. Helper's conversation also calls your own model through Hermes, one or two short calls for a message its fixed rules are not sure of (point 11). These calls spend a little of your model credit too. Nothing is saved to your chats and no tools run.
- Changes nothing in your setup. Where a card suggests a fix, it gives you text to paste into your own chat; you decide and press Send.
- Makes no network calls of its own: no telemetry, no server, no self-update. A source tag ("Hermes docs › …") opens that official docs page in your web browser when you press it.
- Never sends a pasted API key anywhere: the message box checks for one first and shows where keys belong instead. The key is not shown back and not kept.

### What Helper keeps, and where it goes

1. Helper keeps a few things about you without asking first. It tells you each time, in its conversation.
2. It keeps your name, if you give it, and what you do (one short answer).
3. It keeps three kinds of answers you give it: whether you pay for an AI or want a free one, which chat app you use, and one service you named, such as Gmail.
4. It keeps up to three things you ask it to remember. It only shows them to you. It does not act on them, and they are not sent to any AI.
5. It keeps nothing else you type. It tries to refuse passwords, keys and long numbers by fixed rules, but it can miss some. Never type a password or key to Helper.
6. It also keeps a few page settings, such as how wide its panel is, and your answers on its page. It keeps one mark that it has said hello, so it never asks your name twice. Forget all of this does not remove that mark.
7. These are kept as plain text in the Hermes Desktop app's own storage, on this computer only.
8. They are shared by all your Hermes profiles (bots) on this computer.
9. They are not removed when you uninstall Helper.
10. To see and delete what Helper keeps, press "See what I keep" in its conversation, or type "what do you know about me". You can also open Settings, then Plugins, then Helper.
11. Helper answers many messages with fixed text, picked by fixed rules on this computer. When those rules are not sure, your message, the last few lines of your talk with Helper and a few of Helper's checked lines are sent to the AI your Hermes uses: the company whose AI you chose, or the AI on your own computer. Your chats with Hermes go there the same way. That AI writes Helper's reply in its own words from those checked lines, and Helper checks each reply by fixed rules before showing it. On a small AI on your own computer, Helper can still say things that are not true, and the checks do not catch all of them. Nothing is sent anywhere else. Of what Helper keeps about you, only three short lines are sent: what you do, whether you are new to Hermes, and what you want Hermes to do. Your name and the rest never are. When Helper recommends where to start, it also sends what it read about your Hermes setup on this computer, such as whether a model answers and whether you have scheduled jobs. Deleting does not take back what was already sent.
12. Helper has no server of its own. Nothing is sent to its makers.

## Install

Coming with the public release (an install link of the form `hermes://plugin/install?repo=<owner>/<repo>&enable=1`).

## Remove

Capabilities → Plugins → Helper → remove.

## For developers

- `src/` holds the readable sources; `node scripts/build.mjs` joins them into `desktop/plugin.js`, the one file Hermes loads. Edit `src/`, then build.
  - `src/setup-cards.js`: the card rules and fixed words (pure code). `src/setup-tab.js`: the reads and the cards on screen.
  - `src/model-call.js`: the one way the page asks your model (`llm.oneshot`, task `helping_hand`, 90 s) and the fixed line for each way it can fail.
  - `src/key-check.js`: stops a pasted API key before anything goes to a model.
  - Helper's AI step (talk-live). `src/ai-mode.js` holds the one switch `AI_MODE`: `no_ai_step` (nothing typed is ever sent), `ai_picks_line` (the person's own AI picks one of Helper's checked lines) or `ai_writes` (this build, talk-live-2: it writes the reply from those lines, and fixed checks decide whether it is shown). The build prints a note when the content team's data still says another mode, and writes README's four lines that depend on it. `content/helper-talk-live-v2.json` (the content team's) becomes `src/live-data.js`; `src/live-rows.js` picks at most 5 of Helper's checked lines to hand over, `src/live-request.js` makes what is sent (of what Helper keeps only what you do, as one line in the instructions and only when it passes the keep rule again; never the name), `src/live-checks.js` reads the reply: no_reply first on every call (thinking text is no reply; live-v2 changes-5's lists), then the checks c0 to c24, c26 and c27 (c25 is in the data with adopted: false and is not built), `src/live-turn.js` runs one message (code first; the AI only when code is not sure), and `src/page.js` waits at most 12 seconds, with at most one of Helper's calls in Hermes at a time; a call that gave no answer in 12 seconds or gave thinking text, or a message the AI left without an answer for 12 seconds, turns the AI step off until Hermes starts again. When that was the visit's first call, Helper says the slow-AI line SLOW_A once, right after its written answer, with a text to paste (talk-live-3; not after a don't-know line, a line with answers to pick, a question, a goodbye or a line with its own text to paste: then after the next answer; kept for the visit only, never stored).
  - Offers (setup-chat, the content team's changes-6 to -10). A line of Helper's that would leave you to think of what to type carries an offer instead: a second speech bubble with a question and two to four things to pick in the message box (`offers` in `content/helper-talk-v2.json`; `attachOffer` and `pressOffer` in `src/talk.js`; never the same offer twice in a row; typing still works). `src/setup-built.js` holds the one switch `SETUP_BUILT`: while it is `false` the set-up conversation is not in the build and no offer shows the pick "Set up Hermes". New checks on a reply the AI wrote: c28 (it ends on an invitation or a question) and c29 (what you do used as a step or as a fact about Hermes); the label `USED:` is read in any case, and a reply with nothing but the label is no reply. A button or pick you press is shown as your message but is never sent to your AI as your words. A call to your AI that fails turns the AI step off until Hermes starts again.
  - `content/steps-v1.json`: the step texts, written by the Helper content team; the build turns it into `src/steps-data.js`. `src/steps.js`: the rules around it (next step, the "do it for me" words, the hand-over text).
  - Helper's conversation. `content/helper-talk-v2.json`: every line Helper can say, its key words, its answers to pick and what it keeps, written by the content team. The build checks it (`scripts/talk-data.mjs`: every line has a source and keeps the word limits, every answer gives a row of its own topic, no list of things to pick is longer than four, answers only under the questions the team marked in `answers.when_shown`; with a problem the build stops and writes nothing) and turns the fields the code uses into `src/talk-data.js`. `content/helper-wants-v1.json`: what a person may want Hermes to do (24 checked rows in four groups) and the 186 skills of the two official skills pages, written by the content team; the build checks it again (`scripts/wants-data.mjs`: every line within the word limits, every quoted docs line on its named line of `content/docs-lines-v0.23.0.json`, the lines of the Hermes v0.23.0 docs it names, cut by the team's `dev/checks/talk-v2-wants/docs-lines.mjs`; built in or optional as the page says; no command in an optional skill's box; at most four groups of at most eight) and writes `src/wants-data.js`. `src/talk.js`: the fixed order of steps that picks a line, a port of the content team's `route2.py` (a pasted key first, then "remember that ...", "what do you know about me" and "forget ...", "what can I ask", a request to do something, the opening, a skill by its name, then (changes-11) a name typed on a later visit, "what is ..." with the explanation, the cost and where-to-run lines, then key words, the wants' rows, the near miss; the wants steps follow the team's tested order, hhtech's `wants_step.py` with `fixes6_step.py`; one change of the Builder's: a wants row wins over the cost and where lines, as a topic word does). `src/kept.js`: the one stored record of what Helper keeps (key `kept`), cleaned whenever it is read. `src/settings-file.js`: Helper's settings page read back by fixed rules, a port of the content team's `settings_read.py`. `src/settings-page.js`: that page. `src/talk-view.js`: the window's parts, and the things to pick in the message box. `src/bubble-link.js`: the piece between the bubble and the character, one small file so its shape can be swapped. `src/layout.js`: how the page fits its window.
  - `src/talk.css`: the one style sheet of the plugin's own, for Helper's window only. Every class starts with `hh-`, every colour is a Hermes variable, and it has no fonts and no shadows. The build turns it into `src/talk-css.js`; the page puts it in a `<style>` element inside its own page, so it leaves with the page.
  - The character, drawn by the page itself as inline SVG shapes in the accent colour (no picture file): `src/expressions.js` holds the numbers of its four expressions (resting, busy, asking, talking) and the rule for which one shows; `src/character.js` draws it and moves it; `src/eyes.js` turns the pupils toward the cursor or the focused field; `src/reveal.js` shows Helper's lines word by word (`WORD_MS`, 120 ms a word); `src/motion.js` reads the "reduce motion" setting. It is 168 px wide, or 132 px when Helper's window is under 420 px wide or the page under 600 px high (`src/layout.js`).
- The team's files are copied at the checksums each job names (`tests/lib/team-pins.mjs`); when the team's own file has moved on since, the tests print a note and a later job ports it.
- `npm test` runs every test in the committed `AI_MODE`, then again in the other two (`scripts/test-modes.mjs`; the switch is changed only in what the tests load). It runs offline with Node only: build is current, `node --check`, colour lint, plugin shape, model check, card rules, key check, step data checks (sources, word limits, answer cards), Helper's answers compared turn by turn with the content team's own reference script, hhtech's ten typed messages from his newcomer run and "my chat app is WhatsApp" (with his kept record, the wants on), the older "Kept:" lines struck through once forgotten, the wants compared on every message of the team's test sets (296) with hhtech's reference steps and hhcontent's wants fixes layer, hhcontent's 53 wants-fixes cases (`run_wf1.py`), the wants data's build checks (each broken on purpose), the content team's 57 refusal cases, the settings page's read-back compared case by case with the content team's own `settings_read.py`, every record the conversation keeps read back by the settings page, the build checks on the talk data, the style sheet's rules, every stored key against an allowed list (and no browser storage or cookies), the character (each expression's shapes, when each shows, the word-by-word reveal, the eye maths, the rising, reduce motion), and the page, the conversation, the settings page and the tab rendered with stand-ins for React and Hermes.
- `scripts/hermes-checks.sh` (needs a Hermes v0.23.0 checkout and venv): Hermes's desktop lint, `hermes plugins validate`, and a check that every class the page uses exists in Hermes's own built CSS.
- The look: the left side wears Hermes's own UI kit components, plus class strings copied from Hermes's own screens (`src/look.js`, table `K`; each group names its source file). Helper's window wears the classes of `src/talk.css` (table `T`). No colours or fonts of its own anywhere.
- The model's name is shown the way Hermes's own model button shows it ("Mock Small", "Opus 4.8"); on the free tier it says "Nous · free tier". `src/model-name.js` holds those naming rules, adapted from Hermes Agent (MIT License, Copyright (c) 2025 Nous Research, `apps/desktop/src/lib/model-status-label.ts`).

## Known issues

- No `__init__.py` on purpose: it is a desktop-only plugin, so `hermes plugins validate` shows the expected warning "capability probe skipped (manifest-only plugin)".
- The SOUL.md card compares your file with Hermes's own default text (copied from Hermes v0.23.0). If a later Hermes changes that text, an untouched file would show "Works".
- If your SOUL.md exists but Hermes can't read it (for example its permissions), Hermes uses the built-in personality and the card says so; it can't tell that the file is there.
- The pasted-key check also stops a long run of letters and digits that isn't a key (for example a 40-character git commit id). That one message just isn't sent.
- The model-name rules are a copy of Hermes v0.23.0's. If a later Hermes changes how its model button names models, the page can differ until the copy is updated (a test compares the two wherever the Hermes source is checked out).
- Below about 660 px of window with Hermes's left sidebar shown, the open step can't stay 320 px wide even with Helper folded away and the step list as numbers; hiding Hermes's sidebar gives the page the room back.
- In a window 1440 px wide with Hermes's left sidebar shown, the open step is about 464 px wide beside Helper's window, so a step's side-by-side comparison shows as one column. Dragging Helper's window narrower, or hiding Hermes's sidebar, brings the two columns back.
- In a wide window Helper's window cannot be hidden; it can be made as narrow as 360 px.
- On a Hermes without plugin settings pages, Helper's page in Settings is not there; "See what I keep" then shows the list in the conversation, with "Delete all of it".
- Answers to pick go to one column when a label would wrap (at 360 px the four topics, at 460 px the three under "What Hermes can do"), so the message box grows taller there.
